From the collection

It’s not enough to say it looks right.

Presenting your design matters. People need to understand why you made your choices, not just see the finished screen. These are three things I try to remember.

The reasoning

Start with why

When I present a design, I try to explain the problem first. Then I walk through what I changed and why. It helps people follow my thinking instead of having to guess what they’re looking at.

Sometimes I know something feels off, but I can’t explain it right away. That’s when I stop and ask myself why. If I still only have a feeling, I look into it more and do the research so I can explain the choice clearly.

The details

Everything has a reason

You might not believe it, but every button and every word matters. So does the order someone moves through a page. Even a small change can make the next step clearer or more confusing.

As designers, we often know why something belongs there. The harder part is putting that reason into words. I like to go through a screen and ask myself, “What is this helping someone do?” If I can’t answer, I need to take another look.

Consistency

Make it feel connected

Consistency matters too. If the same action looks or works differently from one page to the next, people have to learn it all over again. Keeping the language, buttons, and flow familiar helps them move through the experience with more confidence.

That’s one of the things I check before I present. Does this choice make sense on its own, and does it still make sense with the rest of the product?
